\# Existing E-Governance Systems Review



\## Purpose of the Review



Before deciding the requirements of our system, it is useful to look at existing e-governance and citizen service platforms. This review helps us understand how real systems receive citizen complaints, manage service requests, and keep users informed. The useful ideas from these systems will later help us decide the requirements of our own project.



\## Open311



Open311 is an open standard for reporting non-emergency civic problems such as potholes, street cleaning issues, graffiti, and other local service problems. A service request can contain structured information such as the type of problem, description, location, and a unique request ID.



One useful idea from Open311 is that complaints are collected in a structured way instead of being received as unorganized messages. The request ID can also be used to check the current status of a submitted issue.



For our project, Open311 shows the importance of collecting the issue category, description, location, and tracking information in a clear format.



\## NYC311



NYC311 provides New York City residents with a central platform for accessing city services and reporting non-emergency problems. After a service request is submitted, citizens can use the service request number to check its status.



NYC311 also provides a map where users can view open and recently closed service requests by location, date, or topic. This can help citizens understand whether a problem in their area has already been reported.



For our project, NYC311 shows the value of request tracking, clear status information, and location-based reporting.



\## Bangladesh Grievance Redress System (GRS)



The Bangladesh Grievance Redress System (GRS) is an online government platform where citizens can submit complaints related to public services. After submitting a complaint, the citizen receives a tracking number that can be used with their mobile number to check the progress of the complaint.



GRS can provide updates through SMS or email. Citizens can also see information such as the complaint status and expected resolution date. After a complaint has been resolved, the citizen can provide feedback about the resolution.



For our project, GRS is particularly useful because it shows how complaint tracking, expected resolution time, notifications, and citizen feedback are already being used in a Bangladesh e-governance system.



\## What We Learned for Our System



After reviewing Open311, NYC311, and Bangladesh GRS, several useful ideas can be considered for our system.



Citizens should be able to submit structured information such as the issue category, description, location, and a supporting photo when available. Each request should receive a unique tracking ID so that the citizen can follow its progress after submission.



The system should also provide clear status information and show which department is responsible for handling the request. An expected resolution time and notifications about important changes or delays can make the process easier for citizens to understand.



Location information can also help identify complaints that may be related to the same problem. This could reduce unnecessary duplicate reports. After an issue has been resolved, citizens should also have an opportunity to provide feedback.



These findings will be used as a starting point when defining the functional and non-functional requirements of our proposed system.
