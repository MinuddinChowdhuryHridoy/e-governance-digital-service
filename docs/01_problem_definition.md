\# Problem Definition



\## Current Problem



Citizens often need to report problems such as damaged roads, blocked drains, uncollected garbage, broken streetlights, or water supply issues. These problems can be reported through different methods, such as visiting municipal offices, calling hotlines, using websites, or using mobile applications.



The main difficulty usually starts after the complaint is submitted. Citizens may not know which department is handling their request, whether anyone has reviewed it, how much progress has been made, or why the issue is taking a long time to resolve. Sometimes the same problem may also be reported by several people because they cannot see that another citizen has already submitted it.



Municipal workers can face problems as well. Some complaints may have unclear locations, missing information, or may be sent to the wrong department. Handling many duplicate or incomplete requests can make the overall process slower.



For this reason, the main problem is not simply providing another way to submit complaints. The real need is to create a clear process where citizens can follow their requests from the time they are submitted until the issue is resolved, while municipal departments can manage those requests in a more organized way.





\## Target Users



The system is mainly designed for three types of users:



\* \*\*Citizens:\*\* People who need to report municipal problems, submit service requests, track their progress, receive updates, and give feedback after an issue is resolved.

\* \*\*Municipal Officers:\*\* Staff members who receive service requests, review the submitted information, update the progress of the work, and mark requests as resolved when the required action is completed.

\* \*\*System Administrators:\*\* Authorized users who manage user accounts, service categories, departments, system settings, and overall reporting.



Each user has a different role, but they all use the same system to make communication and service processing easier and more organized.





\## Proposed Solution



The proposed system is a \*\*Municipal Citizen Service Request and Complaint Management System\*\* that provides one place for citizens and municipal departments to manage local service issues.



Citizens will be able to select the type of problem, provide a description and location, attach supporting photos if needed, and submit the request. After submission, the system will generate a tracking ID so the citizen can check the progress of the request.



The request will be sent to the appropriate municipal department based on its category. Officers will be able to review the request, update its status, add progress notes, and provide information when the issue is resolved. Citizens will also be able to see important updates and provide feedback after the work is completed.



The system will also help administrators organize service categories, departments, users, and reports. The aim is to make the whole process easier to follow for citizens and easier to manage for municipal staff.





\## Expected Impact



The expected impact of this system is to make municipal service handling more transparent, organized, and easier for both citizens and city authorities.



Citizens will be able to understand what is happening with their requests instead of being left without updates after submitting a complaint. They can track progress, see which department is responsible, and know when an issue has been resolved.



For municipal departments, having structured information about the issue, location, category, and status can make requests easier to organize and manage. It can also reduce duplicate complaints and help departments identify common problems in different areas.



In the long term, the system can help improve communication between citizens and local authorities, increase accountability, and provide useful data for improving municipal services.
