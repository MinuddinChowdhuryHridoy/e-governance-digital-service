\# Stakeholder Analysis





\## Purpose



The purpose of this stakeholder analysis is to identify the main users of the proposed system and understand what each of them needs. This helps us decide which features and responsibilities should be included in the system before development begins.



\## Citizen



The citizen represents local residents, commuters, business owners, or community members who experience or notice municipal problems. For this project, a typical example is a resident or business owner facing waterlogging caused by a blocked drain, along with a damaged road and broken streetlights in the same area.



The citizen may find it difficult to understand which department is responsible for each problem. They may be redirected between different offices, have to explain the same issue several times, or receive little information after submitting a complaint. Giving an accurate location can also be difficult when the reporting method does not support photos or map-based location information.



The citizen needs a simple way to report the issue from one place by providing a description, location, and supporting photo. After submission, they should receive a tracking ID and be able to see the current status, responsible department, expected resolution time, and important progress updates. Notifications should inform them about major changes or delays. After the issue is resolved, the citizen should also be able to view the resolution details and provide feedback.





\## Municipal Officer



The municipal officer reviews citizen complaints and coordinates with the responsible department or field team. They may face problems such as unclear locations, incomplete information, and duplicate reports. The officer should be able to view complaint details, assign requests, update status, add delay reasons, and mark issues as resolved.



\*\*Main Needs:\*\*



\* Clear complaint details

\* Location and photo information

\* Request assignment

\* Status updates

\* Duplicate report handling

\* Resolution details





\## Department Supervisor



The department supervisor is responsible for overseeing the requests handled by their department and making sure that assigned work is progressing properly. They need a clear view of pending, active, delayed, and completed requests so they can identify problems before they become serious.



The supervisor should also be able to review escalated complaints, check whether officers or field teams are overloaded, and monitor whether requests are being completed within the expected time. Basic reports can help them understand common service problems and overall department performance.



\*\*Main Needs:\*\*



\* View all department requests

\* Monitor pending and overdue cases

\* Review escalated complaints

\* Check workload and progress

\* View service and performance reports



\## System Administrator



The system administrator manages the overall platform and makes sure that the system runs properly. They are responsible for managing user accounts, departments, service categories, permissions, and basic system settings.



The administrator should also be able to monitor system activity, manage access for different user roles, and help maintain the accuracy of service and department information. They may also review system-level reports to understand how the platform is being used.



\*\*Main Needs:\*\*



\* Manage user accounts and roles

\* Manage departments and service categories

\* Control user permissions

\* Monitor system activity

\* Manage basic system settings

\* View overall system reports





\## Stakeholder Needs Summary



The stakeholder analysis shows that each user group has different responsibilities, but they all need a clear and organized service-request process. Citizens mainly need an easy way to report problems and track what happens afterward. Municipal officers need complete information so they can review and process requests efficiently. Department supervisors need to monitor progress, delays, and workload, while system administrators need to manage users, roles, departments, and overall system settings.



These needs will be used in the next stage to define the functional and non-functional requirements of the system.
